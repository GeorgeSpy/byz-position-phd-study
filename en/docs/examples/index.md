### What the proposal examines

Family recognition uses declared musical relations and application criteria. A catalogue relationship is recorded as one item of evidence. Full family judgement and documentation of historical co-exegesis follow their separate protocol procedures.

| Relation | Required documentation | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Catalogue variant | Entry and catalogue conventions | Starting point for examining a candidate melodic type |
| Shared family | Active family specification and independent family judgement | Eligible family supervision |
| Parallel exegesis of a shared passage | Specific work, passage, sources, exegetes and historical relationship | Historical edge for EISE, after family eligibility |

## Example B — Metrophonic equivalence and inverse recovery

The following table is a **schematic explanation of the research problem**. It does not reproduce a particular old manuscript or actual pair of exegeses.

| Old written choice H | Shared metrophonic information M | Additional historical question |
|---|---|---|
| Oligon, in an eligible context | Ascent by one step | Does the analytical development distinguish the choice of sign? |
| Petaste, in the corresponding context | Ascent by one step | How much does prediction of the written choice change when we read the exegesis? |
| Presence or absence of a non-intervallic great hypostasis | The intervallic path may remain the same | Does the analytical form retain a diagnostic trace of the hypostasis? |

The simple baseline knows metrophonia and context. [ATL-INV](../current/inverse-problem.md) adds the actual analytical exegesis, also compares the predefined rule of exegesis and, in paired passages, a second analytical source. The label comes from an independent reading of the specific old witness. The [musicological background (Greek)](../../../docs/musicology/06-old-notation.md) explains why the same metrophonia can correspond to different notation.
