A Byzantine melodic formula (*thesis*) is a variable-length melodic formation whose identity can be examined historically and musicologically. An **occurrence** is located in a particular work, source, and passage. A **family** is not merely an algorithmic cluster: it is defined through a specification that can be applied in independent expert judgements. A **realisation** is the particular melodic form in which a formula occurs.

ECSI investigates the contribution of *structured musicological knowledge* to learning from symbolic sources written in the New Method. Its principal hypothesis is comparative: **when two models have access to the same musical concepts, what additional value comes from composing these concepts according to valid family-signature rules rather than using them as flat supervision targets?**

A complementary question concerns the preservation of musically relevant information: can improved family retrieval be achieved without discarding information about realisations and historical differences?

Four different relationships are distinguished: **same family**, **local passage correspondence**, **historical co-exegesis**, and **document copying/dependency**. None of these relationships automatically entails the others. A separate observation layer records whether an individual diagnostic is PRESENT, ABSENT, UNKNOWN, or UNASSESSABLE.

ECSI does not replace **Studies M1–M5**. It adopts the fixed input representation **R★** from M1, has an estimand distinct from **Δ_hist** in M2, draws on independent musicological judgements relevant to M3, and treats the performance criterion **Γ_F** of M4 as a separate source of external evidence. **Mthesis** and **ATL/ATL-INV** retain their own predictions and historical research objectives.

**Research status:** the specific family specification must be examined and consolidated through source-based signature cards and critical examination of primary sources. It cannot be established solely by a model's numerical performance.
