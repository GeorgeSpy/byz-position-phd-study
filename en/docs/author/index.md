**Research interests:** Symbolic Music Information Retrieval (MIR) · Musicology-informed machine learning · Melodic formula recognition and variation · Representation learning · Computational analysis of historical chant · Scientific imaging and inverse problems

Georgios Spyridakis holds a degree in Physics from Aristotle University of Thessaloniki and an MSc in Music Technology from the Hellenic Mediterranean University (2026). He works as a secondary-school physics teacher in Greece and is a practising Byzantine chanter. His research combines experimental measurement, machine learning and computational representations of music, informed by sustained engagement with Byzantine musical theory and performance.

He is currently developing a **PhD research proposal in symbolic MIR and musicology-informed machine learning**. The project is at the proposal stage, with the Hellenic Mediterranean University as the intended academic setting.

## Current research direction: Byzantine melodic formulas

The proposed doctoral research investigates the Byzantine melodic formula (*thesis*, plural *theseis*) as a computational unit. It addresses the joint segmentation of musical notation and recognition of formula families, the experimental contribution of independently validated musicological specifications, and representation learning from related formula occurrences and historically documented exegeses. Independent musicological evaluation and the descriptive characterization of complete musical works form part of the core research design.

The research plan also describes conditional extensions in auditory categorization, musical expectation, performance timing and comparative analysis of Znamenny chant. These directions examine how musical identity and variation can be represented and evaluated across different sources, performers and chant traditions.

He has studied Byzantine chant since 1998, initially in Thessaloniki and, since 2013, at the Byzantine Music School of the Metropolis of Rethymno. His training follows the teaching lineage of Simon Karas and Lycourgos Angelopoulos. He also sings in the school's choir under E. Kapaidonakis, a founding member of the Greek Byzantine Choir.

This combined background informs the definition of melodic units, the interpretation of notation and the design of musicologically meaningful evaluation criteria. The proposal includes independent expert evaluation to assess those criteria.

## Previous computational research

His MSc research examined automated recognition of instrumental vibration modes from ESPI images. The work combined experimental measurements, physically calibrated generation of synthetic training examples, image denoising, classical and neural classification methods, and tests of generalization across measurement conditions. The corresponding code and research papers are available through the links below.

Subsequent work addresses representation learning and the structural identifiability and inversion of a time-averaged ESPI Bessel observation operator. These studies contribute experience in controlled model comparison, uncertainty, inverse problems and the relationship between physically meaningful structures and learned representations.
