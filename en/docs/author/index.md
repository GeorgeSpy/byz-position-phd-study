Georgios Spyridakis is a physicist and teacher in public vocational education. His scientific work connects physics, music technology, data processing and machine learning. He is also an active chanter, with many years of practice and training in the teaching lineage of Simon Karas and Lycourgos Angelopoulos.

## Research activity

His master's research concerns automated classification of vibrational modes in instruments of the bouzouki family, using machine learning and processing of interferometric images. The associated public code covers physically calibrated synthetic data generation, image denoising, classification and assessment of transfer across measurement conditions.

The continuation of this research includes representation learning from Electronic Speckle Pattern Interferometry (ESPI) data and the study of identifiability and inversion of the [M_ESPI observation operator (Greek presentation)](../../../docs/author/prior-work.md#mespi).

The current [proposal](../../../documents/short-proposal-en.pdf) studies the melodic formula as a computational unit of Byzantine music. It connects chant practice and musicological training with symbolic representation, learning from attested historical relations and probabilistic modelling of variation. Combined experience in musical practice and computational design supports the formulation of musical questions, representations and experiments.

The researcher's role and organisation of independent evaluation are developed in [§5.3 of the Greek short proposal](../../../documents/short-proposal.pdf#page=26).

## Research papers

The [paper collection (Greek catalogue)](../../../documents/prior-work/README.md) includes three papers submitted to SETN 2026, on synthetic supervision, vibrational-mode classification and channel-attention denoising, together with the [full MESPI paper](../../../documents/prior-work/mespi-inverse-geometry.pdf) on identifiability and inverse geometry of a Bessel operator.
