## The musical and computational problem

How can the same melodic formula be recognised when its written form, syllabic distribution and extent of elaboration change? What does an analytical exegesis allow us to recover from the notation of an older manuscript?

The proposal studies these questions in Cherubic Hymn settings and Communion chants. A **melodic formula** is a historically grounded, organised melodic formation of variable length. A **family** is the analytical category to which specific occurrences are assigned using documented criteria. An occurrence is located in a work and source; its realisation describes its particular symbolic form. [The four concepts, with an example](../current/position-and-family.md#a-schematic-example).

## What the dissertation will contribute

**In musicology**, the research will determine which occurrences belong to the same family, which relationships persist through variation and where changes concentrate. It will examine which older notational distinctions remain recoverable in exegeses and how Nikolaos of Smyrna's individuality appears in formula selection and realisation.

**In music information retrieval**, it will establish which representation recognises this musical identity in continuous works, including occurrences that differ in extent and surface form. Alternative acceptable boundaries and hard positive and negative examples become part of the same evaluation.

**In machine learning**, it will test whether the documented historical relationship between two exegeses teaches something beyond their common family label. Comparing correct and perturbed knowledge will establish how expert knowledge and its use influence prediction.

The cognitive study independently examines how family organisation relates to listeners' categorisation and expectation. [What each study will teach us](../review/knowledge-contributions.md) · [Contributions and previous research](../review/contribution-matrix.md).

## The route of musical identity

We first compare symbolic descriptions of the music. Models then learn where an occurrence begins and ends and which family it belongs to, retaining documented boundary ambiguities.

The central experiment examines **what the provenance of a musical relationship teaches**. Under common conditions, it compares learning from same-family pairs with learning from two confirmed exegeses of the same historical passage. The criterion is agreement with contemporary performance variation, measured in independent recordings.

Expert judgements and listening tasks examine whether family organisation relates to human categorisation and expectation. The historical application studies family selection and realisation in complete works by Nikolaos Protopsaltes of Smyrna.

## The historical route and the operator

**Learning historical transformations** examines how one exegesis expands, contracts or modifies a passage relative to another. The aim is to identify regularities that predict variants in families outside the learning material. [Transformations between exegeses](../current/atl.md).

**Historical recovery** asks the inverse question. Different older written choices can share the same metrophonic path: the same reading of the quantitative movements. We test whether analytical exegesis retains an additional trace of that choice. Two co-primary comparisons measure the value of exegesis beyond metrophonia and context, and its additional value beyond known exegetical rules. For passages with two exegeses, the benefit of the second source is measured separately. Predictions are evaluated against an independent reading of the specific older witness. [The inverse problem](../current/inverse-problem.md).

The **probabilistic realisation operator** examines which forms are expected when family and musical context are known. It distinguishes how selective the specification is from which compatible forms occur more frequently. Family identity thereby acquires predictive content for organised variation. [The operator's idea and definition](../current/m-thesis.md).

## From sources to research outcomes

Work begins with a passage inventory and family specifications. It proceeds through independent validation, model comparisons and tests on new works. Neural development enriches the description of variable-length sequences and context.

The wider aim is a methodology for musical traditions in which identity persists across diverse realisations and historical documentation supplies substantive knowledge for learning. Transfer to other modal repertories will be examined using their own musicological evidence and evaluation data.
