The proposal connects **recognition of musical identity** with **the study of historical transmission**. Their common unit is a documented occurrence of a formula in a specific work and source. Family specifications organise recognition and realisation prediction; correspondences between an older witness and an exegesis support historical recovery.

**[Formula, family, occurrence and realisation](position-and-family.md#a-schematic-example)** — four concepts illustrated schematically.

## The research contribution

The research examines **formula identity, the morphology of variation, the information in exegesis and compositional individuality**. It aims to determine which relationships remain stable, which forms characterise each family and what survives of specific older written distinctions.

The computational contribution centres on a practical question: **what musical knowledge does a model need to recognise and predict these relationships?** Input selection, comparison of family and historical supervision, and probabilistic learning of realisations and transformations examine complementary answers.

The cognitive study additionally examines how families relate to listeners' categorisation and expectation. [Research questions and new knowledge](../review/knowledge-contributions.md) · [Contributions and previous research](../review/contribution-matrix.md).

## Questions and methods

| Question | What is examined | Detailed route |
|---|---|---|
| How are formula and family distinguished? | Historical type, analytical category, localised occurrence and symbolic form. | [Definitions](position-and-family.md) |
| How do validity and logical use of musicological knowledge affect learning? | Matched valid/perturbed family specifications and additive/structured use (A2). | **[A2 study guide](../studies/a2.md)** |
| Where do formulae occur, and how are they recognised? | Joint boundary estimation and family recognition, with ambiguous boundaries and abstention. | [Recognition (Greek protocol)](../../../docs/full/05-m1.md) |
| What does the historical relationship between two exegeses teach? | Comparison of historical and family supervision using an independent performance criterion. | [Controlled experiment (Greek)](../../../docs/full/06-eise.md) |
| How does the same passage change between exegeses? | Learning expansion, contraction and modification in documented historical pairs. | [Historical transformations](atl.md) |
| What does exegesis reveal about older notation? | Recovering a specific older choice beyond metrophonia and known rules. | [Inverse problem](inverse-problem.md) |
| What does family identity predict? | A distribution of possible realisations given musical context. | [Probabilistic operator](m-thesis.md) · [Technical note](m-thesis-technical.md) |
| Do learned representations retain historical distinctions? | Predicting older labels from finalised models. | [Historical probing of representations](inverse-problem.md#r4-historical-probing-of-representations) |

The [musicological documentation (Greek)](../../../docs/musicology/index.md) explains formula, variation, metrophonia and exegesis. The [examples](../examples/index.md) connect these concepts with recorded forms and schematic explanations.

The **[ECSI research direction](../research/ecsi/README.md)** develops musicologically grounded family signatures and their probabilistic compilation into a separate MIML/MIR architecture, with its own controlled evaluation programme.
