| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Neume | A graphic sign in musical notation. |
| Atomic event | A unit of the common computational reference layer. Its relationship to neumes is recorded in digital encoding. |
| Melodic formula (*thesis*) | A historically grounded, organised melodic formation of variable length. Historical name, family, occurrence and realisation are distinguished in the [definitions](docs/current/position-and-family.md). |
| Named historical formula | A type named or described in a specified source or analysis. Its correspondence with analytical families is documented. |
| Realisation | The symbolic content of an occurrence in a declared representation and at a specified level of analysis. |
| Occurrence | The localised presence of a formula in a passage of a particular work and source. |
| Formula family | An analytical category with a versioned specification, diagnostic relations and a register of occurrences judged to be members. |
| Catalogued melodic type | An entry in St Anthony's Monastery's *Papadic Formulae* catalogue; the catalogue label and its source provenance are retained. |
| Statistically induced melodic segment | A segment obtained by statistical segmentation of an attested symbolic melody, as in NHPYLM; its relationship to a complete melodic formula is assessed separately. |
| Chunk (as discussed by Mavromatis, 2005) | An analytically identified unit within the family of alternative melodies generated by an HMM for a fixed textual phrase; its organisation is described through decision points (Mavromatis, 2005, §§5.3, 6.2). |
| Family of generated realisations (Mavromatis, 2005) | A set of candidate melodic settings of the same textual phrase produced by the model, distinguished from a historically and musicologically documented formula family. |
| Cluster of induced segments | An algorithmically defined group of segments with a specified similarity criterion, distinct from the work–source groups used for statistical resampling. |
| Work–source group | A splitting and resampling unit that keeps dependent works and related source witnesses together. |
| Family specification (membership specification, family signature) | The diagnostic relations, scope, sufficient alternative configurations and exclusion criteria used to assess family membership. Greek prose consistently uses “προδιαγραφή οικογένειας”. |
| Exegesis | A later, more analytical rendering of an older chant passage. |
| Metrophony | The reading of quantitative neumatic signs as a succession of intervallic movements. |
| Performance | A chanter’s vocal rendering of a written realisation, with its own timing and pitch shaping. |
| Supervision | The information from which a model learns. A positive pair indicates that two examples are connected by the relation under study. |
| Encoder | A model that converts symbolic input into a numerical representation whose distances are used for comparison. |
| Reference model (comparator, baseline model) | A model used as a point of comparison in evaluation. The role covers simple baselines and strong neural models. |
| Reference method | An algorithm or broader approach used for comparison. |
| Reference condition | An experimental configuration used for comparison, such as family-based supervision with matched context. |
| Finalised specification or protocol | A fixed version established before evaluation. Greek prose uses “οριστικοποιημένη προδιαγραφή” or “οριστικοποιημένο πρωτόκολλο”. |
| Frozen parameters | Model parameters or weights that remain fixed during the relevant stage. |
| Reference labels or data (ground truth) | Independently documented evaluation targets, with their provenance and verification procedure recorded. |
| Pilot study | An initial, small-scale feasibility study, termed “πιλοτική μελέτη” in Greek. |

## Variant, variation, realisation and variability

Byzantine chant terminology requires a distinction between **parallagē**, the traditional practice of reading or chanting with solmisation/pitch names, and **melodic variants**, the documented alternative forms of a melodic type. Both senses occur in Greek; context and source provenance determine the meaning, rather than singular or plural number alone.

| English | Preferred Greek and scope |
|---|---|
| *Parallagē* (chant practice) | «παραλλαγή» in its historical pedagogical and theoretical sense; preserve source quotations such as «ἀπὸ παραλλαγῆς». |
| *Melodic variant* / *version* | «μελωδική παραλλαγή» or «εκδοχή» for a specific documented musical form, without assuming a direction of derivation. |
| *Occurrence* | «εμφάνιση»: a located passage in a work and source. |
| *Realisation* | «πραγμάτωση»: the specified symbolic form of a position in a source, distinct from the singer's performance. |
| *Variation* | «διαφοροποίηση» for a process or relationship of differences among forms; retain «παραλλαγή» for specifically documented variants. |
| *Variability* | «μεταβλητότητα» for measured dispersion, including performance timing variability. |
| *Data augmentation* | «επαύξηση δεδομένων» or «τεχνητός μετασχηματισμός», separate from historically attested variants. |

For M1–A2–M2a, prefer **musical identity across distinct realisations**. For M2b use **historical/written differentiation**; for M4 use **performance variability**; for M5 use **within-family differentiation**. The broad English research framing *musical identity under variation* remains appropriate when its estimand is stated precisely.
